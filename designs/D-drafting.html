01 · About

The workshop

BAUVIZ (Visualization in Architecture, Engineering & Construction) brings together researchers and practitioners from visualization and the AEC disciplines to explore how visual analytics, immersive interfaces, and human-AI collaboration can support the digital transformation and circularity of the built environment. The workshop spans the full building lifecycle, from early design and simulation through construction, operation, and reuse, and extends to the scale of districts and urban systems. Within the EuroVis community, it provides a forum for sharing new techniques, identifying emerging challenges, and building interdisciplinary collaborations.

03 · Format & Audience

How it works

BAUVIZ is a half-day workshop. We plan for a format that fosters individual paper presentations as thought-provoking seeds, followed by an interactive discussion with all workshop participants.

Target audience

  • Visualization & Visual Analytics Researchers
  • Computer Graphics & HCI Researchers
  • Architects & Computational Designers
  • AEC Practitioners
  • Civil & Structural Engineers
  • Construction Engineers & Managers
  • BIM & Digital Twin Experts
  • Urban Informatics Researchers

05 · Submission

Call for papers

Short research papers
Original work in the EuroVis short paper format (up to 4 pages, two-column Eurographics style; abstract max. 150 words).
Position / vision papers
Foster discussion and envision future directions in built-environment visualization.
Industrial experience reports
Lessons learned from deploying visualization in real AEC workflows.
Demonstrations
Working systems, tools, or interactive artifacts.
Work-in-progress
Early-stage results open to critique.

Process & policies

All contributions are handled through the Precision Conference System (PCS) via EuroVis 2027 under the BAUVIZ workshop track. Please read and follow the EuroVis 2027 and Eurographics author guidelines, as well as the Eurographics policy on the use of Generative AI tools. Submissions are peer-reviewed by at least two IPC members and one paper chair. Accepted short papers are published in the Eurographics Digital Library (archival) under a CC BY license; at least one author must register for and attend EuroVis 2027 in person.
